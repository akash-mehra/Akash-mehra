I Event horizon 1.27 × 1010 m

Research begins exactly where intuition gives out.

I spent a summer at the Indian Space Lab on a competitive research internship, working on high-level scientific inquiry and complex research methodologies. Nothing at that scale can be reasoned about by feel. You build the method first, you make it survive contact with the data, and you let it tell you what is true.

That is not a different discipline from medicine. It is the same discipline at a different order of magnitude, which is the whole argument this page is making.

II Circuit trace 1.50 × 10−4 m

Then I wanted to build one.

I am an avid programmer, and what holds my attention is the seam: the place where software stops being abstract and starts moving a physical thing. Clinical insight and technical logic turn out to be the same reflex: isolate the variable, reproduce the fault, do not trust a story that the measurement contradicts.

It is why I follow drone systems closely. Autonomous flight is the cleanest case of code, sensors and hardware having to agree in real time, at a frequency where nothing can be hand-waved and the interface is never theoretical.

III Capillary lumen 8.00 × 10−6 m

And underneath every scale of it, a patient.

As House Surgeon I have worked high-volume: intensive rotations in Orthopaedics, and rural postings delivering primary care inside health infrastructure that is critical and genuinely under-resourced.

Volume teaches pattern recognition faster than any other teacher. Rural posting teaches something harder — what happens when the constraint is not the diagnosis but the system around it. That is the point where leadership stops being a title on a badge and becomes logistics, budgets and people.
